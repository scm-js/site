Privacy

Last updated: 27 October 2026

The short version: the editor sends us nothing of what is in your maps, and apart from counting visits and which of its features are used, nothing at all unless you press something that says it will. We keep what an account needs and what a bill needs, and not the contents of your maps or what you typed. The only things counted are visits to our websites, which parts of the editor are used, and downloads of the game graphics, without cookies (section 1), and you can turn the counting off. There is no advertising and nothing that follows you between sites.

1. If you only use the editor

Opening maps, editing them and saving them happens entirely in your browser or on your own machine. Your map files are not uploaded, and we have no way to see them. You can use scmJS forever without us learning who you are.

The pages on this site make two requests to GitHub on your behalf — the release version beside the download buttons, and the plugin list on the plugins page. Those go to GitHub, not to us, and GitHub sees your IP address as it would for any page on their site.

This site, the documentation, the editor at scmjs.dev/editor (and the nightly beside it) and the game page at scmjs.dev/play count visits with a small script of our own. When a page opens, it sends us the page's address, the site you came from, your browser's language and screen width, the version of the editor when the page is the editor, a random number for this page view, and whether this is your first visit today, this week, this month or ever, with the week of your first visit (from the dates described in section 2). Our server keeps the address without anything after a ? except campaign tags (utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign), and with the code in a map, share or join link taken out; it keeps only the name of the site you came from, the main part of your language (en, not en-CA), and your screen width as a range. When you leave the page or switch away from it, the script sends how many seconds the page was in view. Cloudflare adds your country and region.

Our server turns your IP address and your browser's identifying string (its User-Agent) into a code, using a random value that exists only in the server's memory and is replaced every day at midnight UTC. It stores the code and the kind of browser, system and device (for example Chrome, Windows, desktop), not your address or that string. That lets us count how many different people came on one day, but not recognise you the next day, and once the day's value is gone nobody can work out whose code it was. The script sets no cookies and learns nothing about your maps. The desktop app, copies of the editor hosted by other people, and automated browsers send none of it.

The editor at scmjs.dev also counts which of its features are used, with the version of the editor. When you use one, it sends the feature's name and a few words about it from fixed lists: for example that a dialog opened and which one, which layer an edit was on, which plugin's menu item was used (a plugin you installed from your own address is just "other"), or that a map was opened or saved, as which kind of file, with its tileset, its size and the version of the map format. When the editor starts, it sends the language it is shown in, which kind of game data it has and how many plugins are running. When something goes wrong, it sends where in the editor's code the error happened: the kind of error, and the file, line and function for up to six steps leading to it — for an error while drawing the screen, some of those are the parts of the editor it happened in — only for the editor's own code and the plugins we publish (anything else is just "plugin" or "other"). It also says whether your browser was translating the page, since a translator changing the page is a common cause of errors. It never sends the error's message. It never sends what is in the map, its name, a file name or anything you typed. These go with the visit count, under the same daily code, and are kept as long (section 7). The game page counts a replay when one starts playing, with nothing about the replay itself. On this site and the documentation, a click on a link to another site is counted with the address it goes to.

To stop all of this counting in your browser, untick Preferences ▸ General ▸ Count which parts of the editor are used in the editor, or use the button below. Either one stops the visit count too, for scmjs.dev and the editor. It is kept as an entry, scmjs.counting, in your browser, so clearing your browser storage turns counting back on.

While we compare the two, the same pages also count visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which records the page, where you came from, your country, browser and device type, and how fast the page loaded, with no cookies and no identifier. When the editor downloads the StarCraft graphics through our forwarder, we count that download too, with your country and which of our sites asked for it, and nothing else. The switch above does not reach Cloudflare's count; most ad blockers stop it.

This site and the editor at scmjs.dev/editor are served through Cloudflare, which sees your IP address and the page you asked for, as any web host does. Cloudflare also keeps a log of the requests that reach our workers on scmjs.dev and its forwarders (the addresses asked for, a shared map's link among them, and what Cloudflare adds, such as your country) for the few days it keeps such logs. The editor used to be at editor.scmjs.dev; the first time you open it at its new address it copies the settings your browser kept for the old one, your sign-in among them, from one address to the other inside your browser. Nothing is sent to us. When someone pastes a link to a stored map or a shared map somewhere that shows previews (Discord, Slack, a forum), the card shows that map's name, who shared it, its tileset, size and players, and its picture, to anyone who can see the link. Making the card stores nothing and does not count as opening the map.

When the editor saves a map to scmjs.dev, it also sends a picture of the map, which is kept with that revision and counts toward your storage. It is what the map's card is drawn with. If you embed a card somewhere (a forum post, a README, a website), anyone who sees it sees the same things a link's card shows. The card's own address opens nothing; only the link you put around it does.

The editor's scmscx search asks scmscx.com through a forwarder of ours. What you search for passes through it, is kept by Cloudflare for up to five minutes so the same search is not asked twice, and is in the request log above. We do not count it.

2. What is kept in your browser

Under Preferences ▸ Browser storage in the editor, listed under the scmjs.dev plugin:

  • your sign-in session, if you have signed in;
  • a random device identifier, which is what the one free trial per browser is counted against — it is not linked to you and is not an advertising identifier;
  • your AI options and the on/off ticks.

The server keeps the device identifier with your trial (section 7).

On scmjs.dev and docs.scmjs.dev the visit counting keeps one more entry, scmjs.visits: the day, week and month of your last visit and the week of your first. It holds no identifier, and is how weekly and monthly visitors are counted without one. If you turned counting off, scmjs.counting says so. The language you pick in this site's top bar is kept as scmjs-lang.

The editor keeps its own settings in your browser too, the StarCraft graphics it was given or downloaded, and — unless you turn them off — recovery copies of maps with unsaved changes and the copy of a file that a save replaced. None of it is sent anywhere.

Clearing your browser storage removes all of it. The account page at api.scmjs.dev also sets one cookie, described in section 6.

3. What we store on the server

Only once you have started a trial or signed in.

  • Your account. The identifier your sign-in provider uses for you, your display name there, and your email address where the provider says it is verified (Discord and Google do; Battle.net sends none). We use the email only to tell two sign-ins apart as the same person, to reach you about your account, and to fill it in on Stripe's payment page when you buy credit. We do not email you otherwise.
  • A ledger. One row per charge and per top-up: when, how much, and which feature it was for. This is what the activity list on your account page shows you, and it is what a bill is made of.
  • Per-request diagnostics. For each AI request: your account and display name, the feature, the model and its settings, how long it took, how many tokens it used, what it cost, which tools it used, and whether it failed (for a failure, the start of the model provider's error message). Not the content — not your prompt, not your map, not the answer.
  • Your IP address, recorded against a trial when one starts and against your first sign-in. This is the only thing that stops the free credit being collected over and over from one machine, it is used for nothing else, and it is cleared after seven days (section 7).
  • Maps you chose to upload, with their revisions, notes and thumbnails, if you use map storage. Maps are stored in Google Cloud Storage in a private bucket. They are served back only to you, unless you make a link to one: anyone who has a map's link can download that map, without signing in, until you remove the link or the map. The server counts how many times each link is opened. Uploading and making a link are always something you press a button to do.
  • Maps you share, while they are shared. Sharing a map for others to edit sends a copy of it to the server, and every change anyone in it makes goes through the server to the others. The copy, the changes, the names people type to join and the messages they write in the map's chat are held in the server's memory only. They are never written to storage, and they are gone when sharing ends: when the person who shared the map stops, half an hour after the last person leaves, or when the server restarts. People who join from a link need no account. The server's log notes that your account started sharing a map, and when.
  • Maps you keep open. If you choose to keep a shared map open (for a day, a week, a month, or until you end it), it is stored as one of your maps: the file, the changes made to it since its last saved copy, the name of whoever made the last change, and the names of the people who changed it since its last revision, which go into that revision's note. If the server restarts while people are in the map, the names they typed to join are stored until the map is opened again, with what lets each editor reconnect (its colour, a random token, and the account if they were signed in). The chat is still held in memory only and is gone once everyone has left. When sharing ends, the changes and names go; the map and its revisions stay with your other maps.
  • Online games, while they run. A game on the play page goes through the server to the other players and is held in its memory only. The server's log notes that your account opened a game, and when.

4. What we send to the model provider

An AI request is answered by Anthropic, through their API. To answer it we send what the request needs: what you typed, and the parts of the open map that are relevant — which for something like a review or the assistant may be most of the map's contents, and may include a screenshot of the map if you ticked the picture box.

Anthropic processes this under their commercial API terms: API inputs and outputs are not used to train their models. Part of the request — the fixed instructions and reference tables, which are the same for everybody — is held in their prompt cache for up to an hour so that repeat requests cost less; the part of the request that is your map is what makes a request unique and is not shared with anyone.

Our own server does not keep the content of requests. It can be told to keep them for one named account, which we do only when someone asks us to help debug their own requests, and only for as long as that takes.

5. Payment

Credit purchases go through Stripe on Stripe's own page. Your card details are never sent to us and we could not store them if we wanted to. We keep the reference Stripe gives us for the purchase, so a payment can be matched to a top-up, and Stripe keeps whatever their own privacy policy says they do.

6. Cookies

One, on the account page at api.scmjs.dev: it holds your sign-in session. It is HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax and Secure, lasts 60 days from when you signed in, and exists only so that the page knows who you are. There are no analytics or advertising cookies (the visit counting above uses none), so there is nothing to consent to and no banner.

7. How long it is kept

  • Your account and ledger — until you delete the account. Some ledger rows may be kept after that, with the account no longer identifiable, where tax or accounting rules require a record of a payment.
  • Per-request diagnostics — kept while the account exists; they carry no content.
  • Trials — a trial account that never signs in is deleted after 120 days without use.
  • Trial and sign-up IP records — the address is cleared after seven days, the period the limits look back over. What stays is that this browser's device identifier has had its trial and that your sign-in has had its sign-up credit, so each is given only once.
  • Visit counts — about 13 months (400 days), then deleted.
  • Maps — until you delete them or the account.
  • Shared maps — only while they are shared; nothing of them is kept afterwards.
  • Maps kept open — the map and its revisions like any of your maps; the changes and names that go with keeping it open until sharing ends.

8. Deleting everything

The account page has a delete button. It removes your account, your identities, your balance, your ledger, the diagnostics of your AI requests and every map you stored. What stays is the note in section 7 that your sign-in and browser have had their free credit, without your address. It is immediate and it is not reversible — including any credit you have left, so spend or ask for a refund of that first.

You can also ask us for a copy of what we hold, or ask us to correct it, at support@scmjs.dev. We will answer within 30 days. These are rights you have under Canada's PIPEDA and Alberta's Personal Information Protection Act, and under the GDPR if you are in the UK or the EU.

9. Children

The service is not intended for children under 13, and the sign-in providers have their own minimum ages. We do not knowingly keep an account for a child under 13; tell us and we will delete it.

10. Who to ask

scmjs.dev is run by Rebecca Sterling in Alberta, Canada, who is the person accountable for the personal information described here. Anything about your data goes to support@scmjs.dev, and a postal address is available on request.

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. A change that affects what we do with data we already hold will be said plainly here rather than folded in quietly.
